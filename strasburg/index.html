Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Strasburg

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Strasburg

Well before a tank floods a Strasburg garage, it usually shows warning signs. Pay attention to a popping or rumbling sound during heating, brownish hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Ignored, they lead to a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it should be checked promptly.
