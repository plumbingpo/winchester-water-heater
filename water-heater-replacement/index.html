How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • Repairs that keep coming back, one part after another
  • Banging and popping from a tank packed with sediment
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • A tank at or past the eight-to-twelve-year mark
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Replacement?

A water heater replacement involves more systems than most people expect. Gas work covers the supply line, sediment trap, shutoff, and a vent that must draft well enough to keep exhaust out of the house. Electrical work means matching breaker and wire gauge to the element wattage. A correctly rated relief valve has to be piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models pile on more: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

A professional replacement covers the whole installation, not just the tank. Sizing is done by first-hour rating or flow rate. Fuel, venting, and electrical capacity are verified, and whatever expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs get added. Every gas and water joint is leak-tested, gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check, and the old heater is hauled away. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. If your current tank still has good years left and just needs a part, you will hear it from us directly.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
